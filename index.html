01 / ABOUT

Don't let the world shape you. Shape the world.

VOISA is built around a simple belief: young people deserve more than a voice in theory. They deserve accessible information, creative platforms, and practical opportunities to understand the world, question it, and act within it.

Through four main sections, VOISA explores creative and educational areas that empower young people. The People’s Pages shares youth creativity, The People’s Brief makes laws and current issues accessible, The People’s Sound explores music and its history, and The People’s Voices uses annual youth research to show what young people think, experience, and want to change.

VOISA brings together law, literature, art, music, research, and civic action so that expression can become something more than expression — it can become action.

02 / THE PEOPLE'S PAGES

Every voice has a page.

The People's Pages is VOISA's youth literary and creative publication, a space for poetry, essays, short fiction, visual art, photography, and ideas. It is a growing collection of young people's words, perspectives, creativity, giving ideas a place to be shared, explored, and remembered. We are currently accepting submissions for Edition 001: Becoming. Vist The People's Pages section to learn more about the theme, gudielines, and find the submission form. If you know anyone with creative talent or anyone who has any idea or passion to share don't hesitate to tell them this is the place where they can put themselves out there.

WRITER ✦ POET ✦ ADVOCATE

Demma Orellana

VOISA began with a question:

What happens when young people stop waiting to be heard and start creating the world they want to live in?

I created VOISA because I believe having a voice is more than having an opinion. It means having the knowledge to understand the world around you, the courage to question it, and the opportunity to do something about what you find.

I am a writer, poet, student leader, and youth advocate with a deep interest in law, civic action, literature, and the ways people can use their voices to create meaningful change. I have pursued that belief through writing and publishing, leadership in student organizations, community initiatives, and advocacy projects focused on giving young people greater opportunities to learn, create, and participate.

Writing has always been one of the ways I make sense of the world. Poetry gives me a language for things that are difficult to say plainly. Music gives me another kind of expression—one that can comfort, motivate, and connect people without requiring the same words.

My interest in law comes from that same place: a belief that knowledge, strategy, and a willingness to speak up can change the course of someone's life. And that is something worth fighting and living for.

My own writing, including my poetry collection Angry, explores justice, perception, growing up, and the questions that emerge when you begin to recognize the world for what it is— and wonder what it could become.

VOISA is where those pieces of me meet.

Through law, literature, art, music, research, and civic action, VOISA is my attempt to create what I believe every young person deserves: a place where their ideas are taken seriously, their creativity has somewhere to go, and their age is never mistaken for an inability to make a difference.

I don't believe young people are only the leaders of tomorrow.

We are the hope for today.

While the rest of the world is waiting, we are changing.

While the rest of the world is complaining, we are chasing.

And while the rest of the world is angry, we are too.

But we choose
to do something
about it.

— Demma Orellana
